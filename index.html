How it works

The goal

Each level shows a scatter of points. Move the yellow line so it fits them as well as you can, then lock it in. You can drag the round handles to tilt it, grab the line to slide it, or use the sliders.

How the data is made

Every level secretly picks a slope k and an intercept b. Each point is then generated as

y = k·x + b + ε,   ε ~ Normal(0, σ²)

where σ is the level's noise level: the typical distance of a point from the true line. About 68% of points land within ±σ of it, and about 95% within ±2σ.

The three lines

  • Your fit: the line you drew.
  • True trend: y = k·x + b with the noise removed. It is the rule the data came from. In real analysis you never get to see it; here you can, because the game made the data.
  • OLS best fit: the line with the smallest possible mean squared error on these particular points. It is the best anyone can do using only the data on screen.

OLS and the true trend differ because the noise is random. One sample might happen to sit a little high on the right, and OLS follows it. As a result, the true trend's MSE on the sample is always higher than OLS's MSE (it lands near σ²). With more points and less noise, the two lines converge.

Scoring

MSE = average of (yᵢ − ŷᵢ)²
score = round(100 × MSEOLS ÷ MSEyou)
  • Matching OLS exactly gives 100. Twice its error gives 50.
  • You are scored against OLS, not the true trend, because you and OLS see the same data.
  • Each level is worth 0–100 points. Your final rating is based on your average, out of 100.
  • Stars: ★★★ ≥ 95, ★★ ≥ 80, ★ ≥ 60.
  • Meter means the live MSE / R² readout and the residual Lines / Squares view. On meter-off levels both stay hidden until you lock in; after that you can turn them on to review your fit.
  • R² = 1 − (your squared error ÷ squared error of a flat line at ȳ). It can go negative if your line is worse than that flat line.

Hint

Each level has one optional hint. It marks the average point (x̄, ȳ) of the data with a cyan ⊕. The OLS line always passes through this point, so once you know it, you only need to get the slope right.

Using the hint takes 15 points off that level's score, after the score is calculated. For example, a raw 92 becomes 77. The result card shows the deduction.

Levels

Outliers sit 5–7.5 units off the true line, both on the same side, so their pull on OLS is easy to see. Slope and intercept are re-drawn every time you play.
